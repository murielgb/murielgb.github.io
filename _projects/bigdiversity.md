**The Date Palm Big Diversity project** is a large-scale population genomics project exploring the diversity of the date palm (*Phoenix dactylifera*) and its wild relatives. By sequencing hundreds of genomes from across the species range, we investigate how this diversity was shaped through time, from domestication and diffusion to the management of varieties by farmers, and how it is organized in oases today.

### Team and funding

The project was launched when I was a postdoctoral researcher in Michael Purugganan's lab at New York University Abu Dhabi, and was funded through his research grants. It involves numerous collaborators throughout the date palm world, including Jonathan Flowers and Yann Bourgeois, who contributed samples, data and expertise.

### Dataset

The project relies on more than 700 whole genomes sequenced with Illumina short reads, covering cultivated date palms across their range as well as wild relatives within the genus *Phoenix*.

### Research questions

**Structure of diversity.** How is genetic diversity organized across the date palm range, and what does it tell us about the history of the crop?

**Varieties and naming.** How do named varieties relate to genetic identities? We explore clonal lineages, synonyms and homonyms to better understand how farmers name, propagate and exchange date palms.

**Pedigrees.** What are the parental relationships among varieties, and what do they reveal about how new varieties emerge?

**Domestication.** How did the transition from wild to cultivated date palms unfold?

**Diffusion and introgression.** How did the date palm spread across its range, and what role did gene flow with wild relatives play in shaping its diversity?
