**DateBack** is an open-access, collaborative platform compiling archaeobotanical evidence of date palm (*Phoenix dactylifera*) and its wild relatives (*Phoenix* spp.). It was born from a simple observation: although the date palm has been central to oasis agriculture for millennia, there was no comprehensive synthesis of its archaeobotanical record to trace when and where it was first consumed, cultivated and spread.

### DateBack v1: Southwest and South Asia

The first version covers macroremains (seeds, fruits, stems, petioles) from prehistoric to Late Antiquity contexts in Southwest and South Asia. Through an extensive literature review, we assembled 154 entries from 110 archaeological sites, together with 74 radiocarbon-dated entries that refine the chronology.

The platform offers standardized data tables, an interactive map and a full bibliography. It reveals strong regional contrasts in the record: the earliest secure evidence of date consumption comes from the Gulf around 5000 BCE, cultivation appears in southern Mesopotamia and the northern Gulf in the 4th millennium BCE, then in eastern Arabia and the Indus Valley in the 3rd millennium BCE, while in the Levant and northwestern Arabia it is only securely attested by the late 2nd millennium BCE.

Beyond these patterns, DateBack is designed as a resource for future work, from spatiotemporal analyses to the selection of archaeological samples for ancient DNA studies.

### DateBack v2: Africa

We are currently extending DateBack to Africa, with a focus on Egypt and North Africa, in collaboration with archaeobotanist Claire Newton. This second version addresses a long-standing question: since when have date palms, or other *Phoenix* species, been present in Egypt and North Africa?

### Contribute

DateBack is an evolving and collaborative platform. If you would like to share data, report a correction or suggest a reference, please contact us at **dateback@ird.fr**.
